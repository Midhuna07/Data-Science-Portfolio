Ride Demand Forecasting for Indian
Ride-Hailing Services

Forecasting hourly ride demand from 150,000 raw booking records across the Delhi-NCR region (Jan–Oct 2024), comparing three modeling paradigms to identify the most reliable approach for operational deployment.

Approach
  • Data preparation: parsed timestamps, resolved 1,233 duplicate booking IDs, and separated structural nulls (ride never happened) from true missing values before building a clean hourly ride-count series.
  • Feature engineering: calendar features (hour, weekday, month) plus lag (1h–24h) and rolling mean/std features to give tree-based models temporal memory.
  • Modeling: trained Prophet for trend/seasonality decomposition, XGBoost on the engineered feature set, and an LSTM on scaled 24-hour input sequences — all evaluated on the same chronological hold-out (Dec 17–30).

project_01.ipynb — results
Prediction Quality & Feature Drivers
XGBoost tracks the actual hourly demand curve closely across the two-week test horizon, including sharp peak spikes.
Feature importance: the last 24-hour lag and hour-of-day dominate — the model has effectively learned the daily demand rhythm.
Business Implications
  • Driver deployment: shift schedules can be aligned to forecast peak windows (identified around 5–8 PM) to cut passenger wait times.
  • Dynamic pricing: peak/off-peak patterns feed directly into surge-pricing rules.
  • Cancellation reduction: matching driver supply to predicted demand avoids the under/over-supply that drives cancellations.
  • Model choice: XGBoost's feature-driven approach outperformed both Prophet and LSTM on every metric, making it the recommended model for deployment.

Knowledge Graph-Based Discovery of Emerging Research Trends in Artificial Intelligence Literature

Built an end-to-end NLP and network-analysis pipeline over 136,238 arXiv papers to identify research topics, map relationships between papers, authors and topics, and surface emerging research communities.

Approach
  • Corpus construction: filtered 136K papers down to 117,661 AI/ML-relevant records across 5 categories, then drew a stratified 3,000-paper sample spanning 1995–2025.
  • Topic modeling: cleaned abstracts with NLTK, then applied BERTopic to surface 20+ coherent research topics from unstructured text.
  • Knowledge graph: constructed a heterogeneous graph in NetworkX linking 3,000 papers, 2,919 authors, 21 topics and 5 categories (5,945 nodes / 9,000 edges), then ran Louvain community detection to uncover 15 distinct research communities.
  • Applications: built a lightweight recommendation engine that surfaces similar papers and adjacent topics given a single input paper.
